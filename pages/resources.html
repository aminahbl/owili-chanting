Resources

This page gives a jumble of collected chanting books, audio recording repositories, articles and other chanting related material. Please keep in mind that the internet shimmers with continuous change, and a page linked here may move or disappear willy-nilly.

Chanting books

As well as chant text, several of follow books open with rich introductions to chanting, and/or guidance on Pali pronunciation and how to chant.

Daily Chanting, Ānandajoti Bhikkhu
Theravāda chants for daily recital in Pali and English, line by line, from Ancient Buddhist Texts (3rd edition, 2014). Each day of the week has its own sequence, with audio, and the book can be downloaded as PDF, MOBI or EPUB. Some chants on this site take their Pali from it.
Daily Chants, Pa-Auk Tawya Meditation Centre
The chanting book of the Pa-Auk Tawya Meditation Centre in Myanmar, in Pali with English translations (3rd edition), as a PDF from Pa-Auk Thailand. After the daily morning and evening chants, the evening chants follow a cycle of days: the parittas first, then the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta in sections. This edition adds English translations of the Āṭānāṭiya, Girimānanda and Mahāsamaya Suttas, which are chanted weekly.
Chanting for Meditators, Ānandajoti Bhikkhu
A monthly chanting schedule in Pali and English, line by line, from Ancient Buddhist Texts (2016). It can be downloaded as PDF, MOBI or EPUB. The book is based on the one used at Pa-Auk Tawya in Myanmar, and was checked against recordings of chanting made in Myanmar. The evening chants work through the parittas, then the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta in fourteen parts, with further texts from the Abhidhamma. The announcement of the book explains that it was made with meditators in mind.
Bhikkhu Manual
A manual for monks from Amaravati Publications (2020), with a large section of chants. The chant list and the Pali on this site are taken from it.
Mahamevnawa chanting book
A chanting book from the Mahamevnawa monasteries of Sri Lanka, as a PDF. This site uses it as a second source when checking the Pali.
Mahamevnawa Paritta Chanting Book, sample
The opening chapters of the Mahamevnawa Pali–English paritta book (Mahamegha Publishers, 2015), as a PDF. The English is translated from the Sinhala translation by Venerable Kiribathgoda Gnānānanda Thera. The sample covers the preparatory chants and the Mahā Paritta, among them the Maṅgala, Ratana and Karaṇīya Mettā Suttas.
Vandana, Buddha Meditation Centre Winnipeg
A short booklet of devotional chants in Pali and English from the Buddha Meditation Centre of Winnipeg at the Mahamevnawa Buddhist Monastery, as a PDF. It covers the refuges and precepts, the recollections of the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha, homage to the seven Buddhas, offerings, asking for forgiveness and loving-kindness meditation. It opens with a welcome for newcomers and brief notes on Pali pronunciation.
SBS Pali–English Recitations
A recitation book from the monk training centre of Sāsanārakkha Buddhist Sanctuary, as a PDF. It draws on the chanting books of Wat Pah Nanachat, Amaravati and Abhayagiri, and its endnotes record where it differs from the Wat Pah Nanachat book.
The Great Book of Protection, Buddhist Maha Vihara
Pali recitals with English translations, line by line, published for free distribution by the Sasana Abhiwurdhi Wardhana Society of the Buddhist Maha Vihara in Kuala Lumpur (2012), as a PDF. It opens with an essay by Ven. Dr K. Sri Dhammananda on how to chant suttas and a guide to Pali pronunciation. Besides the refuges, precepts and parittas, it includes longer suttas such as the Dhammacakkappavattana, Girimānanda and Mahāsamaya Suttas, and reflections, aspirations and the sharing of merit.
Traditional Pali Chants Book in English
Pali chants with English translations, published by Wat Luang Phor Sodh Dhammakāyārām in Thailand (2011), as a PDF.
Everyday Buddhist Chanting, Acharya Buddharakkhita
Chants translated by Venerable Acharya Buddharakkhita and published by the Buddha Vachana Trust of the Maha Bodhi Society, Bangalore (3rd edition, 2024). This PDF is the edition issued for the International Tipitaka Chanting Ceremony.
Bhavana Vandana, Bhante Henepola Gunaratana
Daily devotions in the Theravada tradition: traditional chants, verses and several longer suttas in Pali and English, on the Internet Archive.

Recordings

This section lists general sources for chanting recordings. Available recordings found for a particular chant are listed in that chant’s info panel.

Wat Marp Jan
Recordings from Wat Marp Jan in Thailand, among them Buddhist Chanting – Morning & Evening Puja: chanting by Ajahn Anan Akincaño and the Wat Marp Jan Sangha, with the morning and evening pūjā, auspicious verses and Thai sarabhañña chanting. The page also offers Dhamma talks and audiobooks.
Pali Tipitaka Chanting (Sinhala Edition)
262 audio files of the Pali Tipitaka chanted in the Sri Lankan style, on the Internet Archive, covering the Vinaya, the four main Nikāyas and the Abhidhamma. The item credits the Tripitaka Chanting Council of Sri Lanka, but the recordings are the Pali chanting of tipitaka.lk, a project of the Path Nirvana Foundation that records the Buddha Jayanti edition of the Tipitaka to be heard alongside its text. The foundation’s dataset release names the chanter as Ven. Mettananda Thero. Whether he chants every section is not confirmed.
Ven. Usgoda Dhammagaru
Recordings of the Pali Tipitaka chanted text by text, listed with the length of each recording, from the Mindfulness Meditation Center in Covina, California. Also available as audio files on the Internet Archive.
Abhayagiri Monastery
Collections of chanting by the Sangha of Abhayagiri Monastery in California, including recordings of its chanting books.
Amaravati Monastery
Chanting from Amaravati Buddhist Monastery in England, set out by the two volumes of its chanting book. The first volume holds the daily chants: the morning and evening recitations, regular reflections such as the Sharing of Merits and the Metta Sutta, and the request for the precepts. The second holds the cardinal suttas, funeral chants and protective parittas.
Mahamevnawa Buddhist Monastery
Playlists of meditation and chanting, and an archive of Pali chants with their text, among them the Maha Paritta, the Ratana, Maṅgala and Karaṇīya Mettā Suttas, and Asking for Forgiveness.
Mahamevnawa Pali chanting on the Internet Archive
A large collection of audio: the Sutta Piṭaka chanted in Pali, chanting of its meaning in Sinhala, the Pirith Potha, and Thai Forest chanting in English.
Malaysian Buddhist Meditation Centre
Audio downloads from a Theravāda vipassanā retreat centre in Malaysia. Aungsi Sayadaw U Pannathamibhivamsa chants the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta and a set of twelve parittas. Sayadaw U Pannanda chants the parittas, Abhidhamma, a morning pūjā and the Anekajāti verses, and there is mettā chanting by the whole group.
BuddhaNet audio chanting
Free audio of chanting from several Buddhist traditions, mostly as MP3. The Pali chanting includes devotional chants, Thai-style chanting led by the abbot of Wat Luang Phor Sodh Dhammakāyārām, and twelve parittas chanted in the Burmese style by Sayadaw U Silananda. There is also Chinese Pure Land, Vietnamese, Nichiren and Tibetan chanting.
Dhamma Earth
Chanting downloads from the Dhamma Earth monastery, whose Pāli Chanting Book follows the chanting book of the Pa-Auk Tawya Meditation Centre.
palichanting.org
An audio library of Pali chants from different traditions, searchable by name or by categories such as country of origin. Where possible, each chant links to the Pali text being chanted. The site also lists chanting books and gives chanting tips.

Articles and talks

Chanting as a Practice, Ajahn Kevali
An exploration of the value of chanting adapted from a Dhamma talk given at Wat Pah Nanachat after morning chanting on 18 October 2010. It includes discussion on chanting the parittas as a meditation, and on recalling the meaning of the words while reciting them.
Chanting as a Practice 2, Ajahn Kevali
A short reflection adapted from the book Seeing the Way, Volume 2. Chanting can be used as a way to build concentration, by reciting precisely and keeping attention on the chant from start to end.
Chanting as Practice, Ajahn Pasanno
A recorded morning reflection given on 1 June 2018, from Abhayagiri Monastery’s collection of talks.
Using Chanting to Steady the Mind, Ajahn Sucitto
A talk of about 30 minutes given on 17 December 2019 during a retreat at Uttama Bodhi Vihara, from Dharma Seed. Untrained mental energy is ragged, sluggish and incoherent, and the simple tones and slow, repetitive rhythm of chanting can steady it.
Chanting Brings Breath and Sound Together, Ajahn Sucitto
A talk of about 45 minutes given on 5 February 2020 during the retreat Firm Center, Open Heart at Dharmagiri, from Dharma Seed. Chanting spreads energy through the body and the space around it, and its sound can carry the energy of the heart outwards. The talk includes a practice of bringing breath and sound together, an overview of Pali pronunciation and an explanation of pūjā.

The early Buddhist texts in Pali and other languages, with translations. The references on the chant pages link to it.

Readings and discussions of Pali suttas, with word-by-word analysis. Some chant pages link to the reading of their text.
