Resources

This page gives a jumble of collected chanting books, audio recording repositories, articles and other chanting related material. Please keep in mind that the internet shimmers with continuous change, and a page linked here may move or disappear willy-nilly.

Chanting books

Bhikkhu Manual
A manual for monks from Amaravati Publications (2020), with a large section of chants. The chant list and the Pali on this site are taken from it.
Mahamevnawa chanting book
A chanting book from the Mahamevnawa monasteries of Sri Lanka, as a PDF. This site uses it as a second source when checking the Pali.
Daily Chanting, Ānandajoti Bhikkhu
Theravāda chants for daily recital in Pali and English, line by line, from Ancient Buddhist Texts (3rd edition, 2014). Each day of the week has its own sequence, with audio, and the book can be downloaded as PDF, MOBI or EPUB. Some chants on this site take their Pali from it.
SBS Pali–English Recitations
A recitation book from the monk training centre of Sāsanārakkha Buddhist Sanctuary, as a PDF. It draws on the chanting books of Wat Pah Nanachat, Amaravati and Abhayagiri, and its endnotes record where it differs from the Wat Pah Nanachat book.
Traditional Pali Chants Book in English
Pali chants with English translations, published by Wat Luang Phor Sodh Dhammakāyārām in Thailand (2011), as a PDF.
Everyday Buddhist Chanting, Acharya Buddharakkhita
Chants translated by Venerable Acharya Buddharakkhita and published by the Buddha Vachana Trust of the Maha Bodhi Society, Bangalore (3rd edition, 2024). This PDF is the edition issued for the International Tipitaka Chanting Ceremony.
Bhavana Vandana, Bhante Henepola Gunaratana
Daily devotions in the Theravada tradition: traditional chants, verses and several longer suttas in Pali and English, on the Internet Archive.

Recordings

This section lists general sources for chanting recordings. Available recordings found for a particular chant are listed in that chant’s info panel.

Ven. Usgoda Dhammagaru
Recordings of the Pali Tipitaka chanted text by text, listed with the length of each recording, from the Mindfulness Meditation Center in Covina, California.
Abhayagiri Monastery
Collections of chanting by the Sangha of Abhayagiri Monastery in California, including recordings of its chanting books.
Amaravati Monastery
Talks and chanting from Amaravati Buddhist Monastery in England.
Mahamevnawa Buddhist Monastery
Playlists of meditation and chanting, and an archive of Pali chants with their text, among them the Maha Paritta, the Ratana, Maṅgala and Karaṇīya Mettā Suttas, and Asking for Forgiveness.
Mahamevnawa Pali chanting on the Internet Archive
A large collection of audio: the Sutta Piṭaka chanted in Pali, chanting of its meaning in Sinhala, the Pirith Potha, and Thai Forest chanting in English.
palichanting.org
An audio library of Pali chants from different traditions, searchable by name or by categories such as country of origin. Where possible, each chant links to the Pali text being chanted. The site also lists chanting books and gives chanting tips.

Articles and talks

Chanting as a Practice, Ajahn Kevali
An exploration of the value of chanting adapted from a Dhamma talk given at Wat Pah Nanachat after morning chanting on 18 October 2010. It includes discussion on chanting the parittas as a meditation, and on recalling the meaning of the words while reciting them.
Chanting as a Practice 2, Ajahn Kevali
A short reflection adapted from the book Seeing the Way, Volume 2. Chanting can be used as a way to build concentration, by reciting precisely and keeping attention on the chant from start to end.
Chanting as Practice, Ajahn Pasanno
A recorded morning reflection given on 1 June 2018, from Abhayagiri Monastery’s collection of talks.

Texts and study

SuttaCentral
The early Buddhist texts in Pali and other languages, with translations. The references on the chant pages link to it.
Pāli Sutta Readings
Readings and discussions of Pali suttas, with word-by-word analysis. Some chant pages link to the reading of their text.

Tools

PTS converter
Converts references to the Pali Text Society editions, such as M.I.10, to sutta numbers, such as MN 2.
