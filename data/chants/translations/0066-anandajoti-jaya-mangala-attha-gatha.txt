[chant:0066 jaya-mangala-attha-gatha]
# Bāhuṁ sahassam-abhinimmita sāvudhan-taṁ
1 Māra, the terrible one, who had created a thousand arms with weapons,
# Grīmekhalaṁ udita-ghora-sasena-māraṁ
2 and had mounted on (the elephant) Girimekhala, with his army,
# Dān'ādi-dhamma-vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
3 did the lord of sages defeat by such things as generosity and so forth -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
4 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Mārātirekam-abhiyujjhita-sabba-rattiṁ
5 Even more terrible than Māra was the impatient and obdurate yakkha Ālavaka,
# Ghoram-pan'āḷavakam-akkhama-thaddha-yakkhaṁ
6 who fought (with the Buddha) throughout the whole night,
# Khantī-sudanta-vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
7 (him) did the lord of sages defeat by his patience and self-control
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
8 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Nāḷāgiriṁ gaja-varaṁ atimatta-bhūtaṁ
9 The great elephant Nālāgiri, who was completely intoxicated,
# Dāv'aggi-cakkam-asanīva sudāruṇan-taṁ
10 fearful, like a jungle fire, a flaming discus, or a flash of lightning,
# Mett'ambu-seka-vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
11 did the lord of sages defeat by sprinkling (him) with the waters of friendliness -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
12 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Ukkhitta-khaggam-atihattha-sudāruṇan-taṁ
13 With a sword that was lifted high in his outstretched hand, that very fearsome (bandit)
# Dhāvan-ti-yojana-path'aṅguli- mālavantaṁ
14 the one with a finger necklace (Aṅgulimāla) ran for (the whole of) three leagues up the path:
# Iddhī'bhisaṅkhata-mano jitavā mun'indo
15 (him) did the lord of sages defeat with the psychic powers created in his mind -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
16 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Katvāna kaṭṭham-udaraṁ iva gabbhinīyā
17 Having made her midriff (look large) with a piece of wood, like one who is pregnant,
# Ciñcāya duṭṭha-vacanaṁ jana-kāya majjhe
18 Ciñcā (then spoke) wicked words in the midst of a group of people:
# Santena soma-vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
19 (her) did the lord of sages defeat by means of his gentleness and peacefulness -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
20 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Saccaṁ vihāya-mati-saccaka-vāda-ketuṁ
21 The arrogant Saccaka, who had given up the truth, and had challenged (the Buddha) to a debate,
# Vādābhiropita-manaṁ ati-andha-bhūtaṁ
22 with his mind fixed on his argument, completely blind to the truth,
# Paññā-padīpa-jalito jitavā mun'indo
23 (him) did the lord of sages defeat with the shining lamp of true wisdom -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
24 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Nandopananda-bhujagaṁ vibudhaṁ mah'iddhiṁ
25 Taming the wise dragon Nandopananda, who had great power,
# Puttena thera-bhujagena damāpayanto
26 by way of his son (Moggallāna), who was a dragon of an elder,
# Iddhūpadesa-vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
27 (thus) did the lord of sages defeat (him) by instructing (his son to use) psychic power -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
28 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Duggāha-diṭṭhi-bhujagena sudaṭṭha-hatthaṁ
29 He whose hand was badly bitten by the serpent of wrongly taken up views,
# Brahmaṁ visuddhi-jutim-iddhi-bakābhidhānaṁ
30 the brahma Baka, who was clear and bright, and who possessed psychic powers,
# Ñāṇāgadena vidhinā jitavā mun'indo
31 did the lord of sages defeat with the medicine of his super-knowledge -
# Tan-tejasā bhavatu te jaya-maṅgalāni
32 through that resplendent power may you have the blessings of success!
# Etā pi buddha-jaya-maṅgala-aṭṭha-gāthā
33 He who recites these eight verses each and every day,
# Yo vācano dina-dine saratem-atandī
34 which are about the Buddha’s (many) blessed successes, and remembers them with diligence
# Hitvān'aneka-vividhāni c'upaddavāni
35 after giving up (all) the many and various kinds of adversity,
# Mokkhaṁ sukhaṁ adhigameyya naro sapañño
36 that wise man will (soon thereafter) attain both freedom and happiness.
