# Translation guide

The English on this site is its own translation of the Pali. This guide sets the principles every translation follows and the fixed renderings of recurring terms, so that all the chants read as one consistent translation. A change of principle or rendering is made here first, then carried into the chants.

## Principles

### Faithful to the Pali

The English says what the Pali says. It is not overly creative, and it does not add interpretations the text does not support. Where a word has several valid meanings, the choice is made among those meanings, not beyond them.

### Written for chanting

The words are recited as devotion and reflection, often together, not read as a study text. The English is written to be read alongside the chanting and to carry the same feeling.

### Drawing out the Buddha's kindness and compassion

Where the Pali allows a choice, the rendering that draws out the Buddha's care for all beings is preferred. This works within the first principle:

- **Choosing among valid meanings.** *Mānasa* can be "mind" or "heart". In the Mettā Sutta and in "pacchimā-janatānukampa-mānasā" (Dedication of Offerings), "heart" is chosen.
- **Not adding words.** "Buddho" is "the Buddha" or "awakened", never "the loving Buddha". Warmth comes from word choice and rhythm, not from added adjectives.
- **Keeping the address personal.** Where the Pali addresses the Buddha ("bhante bhagavā"), the English addresses him directly ("Venerable Lord").
- **Room for judgement.** *Sārathi* is a charioteer, one who guides and trains. "Guide" is valid, and so is "tamer", which can bring in calming imagery.

### One English line for each Pali line

Each English line renders its own Pali line, so the two can be shown together in the text view and on a slide.

Pali word order often puts the verb of a verse in a later line than its subject. The English distributes the sentence across the lines so that each line still corresponds to its Pali line, rather than moving all the meaning into one line. Every translation must also read well and make sense on its own, so the two aims are balanced.

Example, from the Karaṇīya Mettā Sutta:

| Pali | English |
|---|---|
| Mātā yathā niyaṁ puttaṁ | Just as a mother would her own son, |
| Āyusā eka-puttam-anurakkhe | guarding him with her very life, |
| Evam-pi sabba-bhūtesu | so too, towards all beings, |
| Mānasam-bhāvaye aparimāṇaṁ | let one cultivate a boundless heart. |

### Plain, warm and dignified register

- Modern English, without archaic forms such as "thee", "thou" or "hath".
- One exception is kept for familiarity: the opening formula "Evaṁ me sutaṁ" is "Thus have I heard."
- Pronouns for the Buddha are not capitalised ("he", "his"). The devotional convention of "He" and "His" reads as old-fashioned.

### Short enough to read at a glance

On a slide the English sits under a large Pali line and must be read within the time it takes to chant that line. A rendering that is accurate in fewer words is preferred over a longer one.

### Explanations go in the notes

Explanations do not go into the English line, because they lengthen it and blur the line-by-line correspondence. Where a line needs explaining, the explanation goes into the chant's `notes`, which the chant page shows in its info popover.

### Men's and women's forms

Where the forms differ only in grammatical gender (for example "anatīto" and "anatītā", both "not gone beyond"), the English is the same for both. A different English line is written only if the meaning differs.

## Pali words in the English

- **Kept, with diacritics as in the Pali text:** Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, Tathāgata, deva, and the names of people and places.
- **Kept, in their English dictionary spelling without diacritics:** samadhi, jhana, nibbana, bhikkhu, bhikkhuni.
- **Translated:** every other word, following the renderings below.

## Fixed renderings

The same Pali is translated the same way in every chant, so that a user who knows one chant recognises the phrase in another. When a new recurring term appears, its rendering is added here before the chant is translated.

**Key terms:**

| Pali | English |
|---|---|
| *buddha*, *sambuddha*, *bodhi* | awakened, awakening |
| *dukkha* | suffering |
| *puñña* | goodness |

"Awakened" follows the root meaning of *budh*, to wake up, and is used by many recent translators. "Suffering" is the most familiar rendering of *dukkha*. "Goodness" is used for *puñña* because it is warmer and clearer than the established "merit".

**Qualities of the Buddha** (Iti pi so):

| Pali | English |
|---|---|
| *bhagavā* | the Gracious One |
| *arahaṁ* | worthy |
| *sammā-sambuddho* | perfectly self-awakened |
| *vijjā-caraṇa-sampanno* | accomplished in knowledge and conduct |
| *sugato* | well-gone |
| *lokavidū* | knower of the worlds |
| *anuttaro purisa-damma-sārathi* | unsurpassed guide of those ready to be trained |
| *satthā deva-manussānaṁ* | teacher of devas and humans |

The formula as lines:

| Pali | English |
|---|---|
| Iti pi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammā-sambuddho | Such indeed is the Gracious One: worthy, perfectly self-awakened, |
| Vijjā-caraṇa-sampanno sugato lokavidū | accomplished in knowledge and conduct, well-gone, knower of the worlds, |
| Anuttaro purisa-damma-sārathi | unsurpassed guide of those ready to be trained, |
| Satthā deva-manussānaṁ buddho bhagavā | teacher of devas and humans, awakened, the Gracious One. |

## Making and reviewing a translation

1. Each chant is translated line by line from the Pali, using this guide. Other translations and dictionaries, such as the [Digital Pāli Dictionary](https://dpdict.net/), may be consulted for meaning; the wording is our own.
2. Recurring terms take their rendering from the tables above. A new recurring term is added to the tables first.
3. Lines where the translation is uncertain (a rare word, a disputed reading, or a choice that may lean too far towards warmth) are listed with the reason when the chant is handed over for review, so review can start there.
4. A new translation is marked `translation: "draft"` in the chant's data. After review it is marked `"reviewed"`.
